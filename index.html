01 - Why Third Places?

Agora - Third Places

When people talk about what makes cities feel vibrant, they rarely mention infrastructure or technology.

They talk about their favorite café, the bookstore they visit every weekend, or the park where they unexpectedly met someone new.

These spaces, often called third places, exist outside of home and work and play a critical role in building community, creativity, and belonging.

Yet despite growing interest in meaningful social connection, these spaces are becoming increasingly difficult to find, sustain, and incorporate into everyday life.

Our team set out to explore why.

"What if the most important places in your life aren't where you live or work, but the spaces in between?"

01 - Why Klinos?

Klinos

Healthcare isn't broken from a lack of services. The doctors, specialists, and insurance are all there. What's missing is coordination between them.

Patients are often left managing referrals, transferring records, scheduling appointments, and navigating disconnected providers on their own. Even individuals with premium insurance and access to top healthcare systems experience delays, duplicated tests, and fragmented care journeys.

Klinos was founded to solve this problem by creating a vertically integrated healthcare platform that combines longevity medicine, advanced prevention, and on-demand care under one coordinated experience.

02 - The Challenge

During discovery, one challenge became immediately apparent: users didn't understand what made Klinos different.

Most healthcare companies focus on individual services, while Klinos was offering something much larger, a coordinated healthcare ecosystem designed around prevention and long-term wellbeing.

The challenge wasn't simply presenting information. It was helping users understand how multiple healthcare services could work together to create a better experience.

How might we communicate a complex healthcare model in a way that feels intuitive, trustworthy, and valuable?
03 - Understanding the User

Through stakeholder discussions, market research, and competitor analysis, I identified a recurring pattern. People weren't frustrated by a lack of healthcare options, they were frustrated by the effort required to coordinate everything themselves.

Managing appointments, referrals, specialists, records, and preventative screenings often felt like a full-time job.

People don't need more healthcare services. They need a better way to navigate them.

This shifted the focus from promoting services to designing a system that made healthcare feel simpler and more connected.

04 - Designing the Ecosystem

A single touchpoint wouldn't support the entire healthcare journey. Users needed a place to learn about Klinos and begin their journey, and a platform that could support them after onboarding and help them stay engaged with their health over time.

To address these needs, I designed both a website experience and a companion mobile application, each serving a unique role within the ecosystem.

🌐
Website Experience

The first introduction to Klinos, focused on educating, building trust, and communicating the value of coordinated healthcare through outcomes rather than medical terminology.

📱
Mobile App Experience

A centralized hub where members access health information, manage appointments, view recommendations, and stay connected with their care team.

05 - Website Design Goals

Transforming a complex healthcare model into a story users can easily understand and act on.

  • Simplifying complex healthcare offerings into clear service pathways
  • Building trust through educational content and transparent messaging
  • Creating intuitive navigation across multiple healthcare programs
  • Guiding users toward consultations and membership enrollment
06 - Mobile App Experience

While the website introduces users to Klinos, the mobile app supports them throughout their ongoing healthcare journey, reducing fragmentation and helping members stay engaged with their health over time.

📊
Health Dashboard

Personalized overview of health status and goals.

📅
Appointments

Scheduling and management across providers.

🗂️
Records & Results

Health records and screening results in one place.

💬
Care Coordinator

Direct communication with the care team.

07 - Key Design Decisions
🎯
Outcomes Over Services

Structured content around user goals and health outcomes, so users understand not just what Klinos offers, but why it matters.

🧩
Simplifying Information

Used progressive disclosure, clear hierarchy, and simplified language without sacrificing credibility.

🤝
Trust Through Transparency

Educational content and straightforward navigation helped users feel confident in their decisions.

📐
Designing for Scale

A reusable design system supports consistency across web and mobile, and enables future growth.

08 - Outcome & Impact

The final experience transformed a complex healthcare business model into a clear and approachable digital ecosystem, communicating a unified vision of prevention, longevity, and coordinated care. The website educates and converts prospective members, while the mobile app supports ongoing engagement and health management.

  • Established a unified digital ecosystem across web and mobile touchpoints
  • Improved understanding of healthcare services through simplified navigation and content architecture
  • Built a scalable design system supporting future products and platform growth
  • Strengthened trust through clear, educational design
09 - What I Learned
"This project taught me that some of the most important design challenges are not interface problems, they are communication problems. The challenge wasn't designing a healthcare website or a mobile app, it was helping people understand an entirely new way of experiencing healthcare."

By focusing on coordination rather than individual services, I was able to design an ecosystem that simplifies complexity, builds trust, and supports users throughout their healthcare journey. Great design isn't just about making information available, it's about helping people understand what matters, why it matters, and what action they should take next.

01 - Why Best Buy Connect?

Best Buy Connect

Redesigning Internal Communication by Listening to What Employees Actually Needed

Large organizations rely on internal communication tools to keep employees informed, connected, and engaged.

At Best Buy, the existing employee platform served as a central hub for company information, but adoption remained low. Employees were increasingly turning to external tools and informal channels to communicate, share knowledge, and build community.

Our team set out to explore how Best Buy could create a more engaging internal experience that better supported employee communication and connection.

🏢
Client
Best Buy (Class Project)
⏱️
Timeline
12 Weeks
🎨
My Role
UI/UX Designer & Researcher
🧭
Platform
Web Application
02 - The Challenge

When the project began, we assumed the problem was rooted in improving communication inside the store environment. Like many internal tools, the initial focus was on making information more accessible and improving day-to-day workflows.

However, after speaking directly with employees, we realized we were solving the wrong problem. The real challenge was not communication during work, it was communication outside of work.

Employees wanted a space where they could connect with peers, share experiences, ask questions, and build relationships beyond their immediate teams. This insight completely changed the direction of the project.

How might we create a platform that fosters community and connection across the entire Best Buy employee network?
03 - Research & Discovery

Through user interviews, competitive analysis, and collaborative workshops, we explored how employees currently communicated and where existing tools were falling short. One theme emerged repeatedly: employees wanted connection, not just communication.

Most internal platforms focused on company announcements and top-down messaging, while employees were looking for opportunities to learn from one another, participate in discussions, and engage with colleagues across locations. This finding became the foundation of the redesign.

🔍
Competitive Analysis

Benchmarked Slack, Microsoft Teams, Meta Workplace, and Yammer to understand what made workplace communities succeed.

🎤
User Interviews

Employees wanted community and peer connection, not just top-down announcements from management.

🔄
The Pivot

Interview findings refocused the project from in-store tooling to an out-of-store community platform.

📋
CPS Framework

Morphological grids, dot voting, and decision charts helped the team evaluate and converge on ideas.

04 - Reframing the Experience

Instead of creating another information-sharing tool, we shifted our focus toward building a community platform. The goal was to support both company communication and employee-driven interaction.

People didn't need another channel for announcements. They needed a reason to open the app between shifts.

To better understand successful workplace communities, we analyzed platforms such as Slack, Microsoft Teams, Workplace, and Yammer. While each platform supported collaboration, we identified an opportunity to create a more community-centered experience tailored specifically to Best Buy employees.

05 - The Design System

With the direction set, we built a lightweight design system to keep the experience consistent across feeds, groups, and announcements, while giving the platform a warmer, more social feel than a typical corporate intranet.

06 - The Solution

The redesigned platform transforms Best Buy Connect into a space where employees can stay informed, build relationships, and share knowledge across teams and locations.

🏠
Community Feed

A central homepage surfaces company updates, team highlights, employee achievements, and ongoing conversations, creating a more dynamic and engaging experience.

👥
Groups & Communities

Employees can join discussion groups based on departments, interests, locations, or shared experiences, making it easier to connect beyond their immediate workplace.

📢
Announcements

Official updates remain accessible through dedicated manager-led communication channels, ensuring important information is easy to find without overwhelming community discussions.

🔒
Employee Privacy Controls

Recognizing that not every employee wants the same level of visibility, the platform includes customizable profile and privacy settings, allowing users to control how they participate.

07 - Final Screens
08 - Outcome & Impact

The redesigned concept shifted Best Buy Connect from a static information portal to a community-driven platform. By focusing on peer connection rather than solely company communication, the experience better aligned with what employees actually valued and needed.

  • Reframed the platform around peer community rather than top-down announcements
  • Designed a groups system that lets employees connect across departments and locations
  • Separated official communication from community discussion to reduce noise
  • Introduced privacy controls that respect varying comfort levels with visibility

More importantly, the project demonstrated how research can uncover opportunities that are not immediately visible at the start of a design process.

09 - What I Learned
"Listening to users is the most important step in creating a solution that truly works. The pivot we made based on interview findings was the defining moment of the whole project."

This project reinforced one of the most important lessons in UX design: assumptions are rarely as valuable as user insight. The defining moment wasn't designing new features, it was realizing that our original problem statement was incomplete.

01 - Why Rebrand Cozymeal?

Cozymeal

Reimagining a Culinary Marketplace Through a More Human and Memorable Brand Identity

While the platform offered a wide range of experiences, its brand identity did not fully capture what made those experiences special.

The challenge was not simply creating a new logo.

It was creating a brand that reflected the warmth, connection, and sense of discovery that people experience when sharing food with others.

As a solo designer, I led a complete rebrand that included a new visual identity, website redesign, merchandise system, marketing assets, and brand guidelines.

02 - The Challenge

Cozymeal's existing identity leaned heavily on stock food photography and generic marketplace styling, the kind of look that could belong to almost any food delivery or booking app.

But Cozymeal isn't just a marketplace, it's a way for people to gather, learn, and share a meal together. The brand needed to feel as warm and human as the experiences it was selling.

How might we design a brand identity that feels as personal and inviting as sitting down to a home-cooked meal?
03 - Brand Strategy

Before touching any visuals, I defined a set of brand principles to guide every design decision: warm over corporate, crafted over generic, and personal over transactional.

🍽️
Human, Not Corporate

Visuals that feel like an invitation from a friend, not a faceless platform.

✏️
Crafted Details

Hand-touched textures and illustrative elements that nod to culinary craft.

🧡
Warm Palette

A palette inspired by spice, citrus, and tabletop tones rather than sterile app blues.

🔁
Memorable System

A flexible identity that scales from app icons to packaging and merchandise.

04 - Logo & Identity

The new logo mark draws from the idea of a table setting, simple, geometric, and instantly recognizable at any size. Paired with a warm, rounded wordmark, the identity feels friendly without losing credibility.

05 - Color Palette

The palette draws from fresh produce and warm tabletop tones, grounded, inviting, and distinct from the cool blues typical of marketplace apps.

06 - Print & Merchandise

The identity was extended into real-world applications, packaging for chef kits, branded aprons and totes, and printed menus, to test how the system held up beyond the screen.

07 - Social Media Design

A set of social templates brought the new identity into Cozymeal's day-to-day content, making the brand feel cohesive across every post, story, and promotion.

📱
Post Templates

Reusable layouts for chef spotlights, recipes, and event promotions.

🎞️
Story Highlights

Custom icon covers consistent with the new visual system.

🖼️
Campaign Visuals

Promotional graphics for seasonal experiences and launches.

🏷️
Brand Voice

Tone and messaging guidelines to match the warmer visual identity.

08 - Outcome & Impact

The refreshed identity gave Cozymeal a distinct visual presence, one that feels personal, crafted, and easy to recognize across digital and physical touchpoints.

  • Developed a cohesive identity system spanning digital, print, and merchandise
  • Replaced generic marketplace visuals with a warm, human brand language
  • Created a flexible logo and color system that scales across applications
  • Extended the brand into real-world touchpoints through packaging and social templates
09 - What I Learned
"A brand identity isn't just a logo, it's the feeling someone gets before they even read a word. For Cozymeal, that meant designing for warmth first and letting every detail follow from there."

This project reinforced how much small details, texture, color, type pairing, shape language, compound into a brand's overall feel. Designing system-first, rather than asset-first, made it possible to keep that feeling consistent everywhere the brand showed up.

01 - The Forgotten Footage Problem

HeroFlow for Lemonlight Studios

Video production agencies generate enormous amounts of content.

For Lemonlight Studios, years of client work had resulted in thousands of hours of footage spread across campaigns, brands, and projects.
While this archive held immense value, most of it remained effectively inaccessible.

Teams often knew the footage existed. They just couldn't find it. What should have been a competitive advantage had become a bottleneck.

🎨
My Role
Product Designer & Researcher
👥
Team
5-Person Cross-Disciplinary
⏱️
Timeline
5-Day Product Sprint
📦
Deliverables
Product Strategy · Prototype · Pitch Deck
02 - The Challenge

During stakeholder interviews, a recurring theme quickly emerged: the organization's search system wasn't built around how people think. Instead of searching for footage naturally, users needed to remember project names, folder structures, or rely on editors who knew where content lived.

"I know we already shot something like this, but finding it takes longer than creating something new."

The problem wasn't a lack of content. It was a lack of accessibility.

How might we help creative teams instantly discover, understand, and reuse existing footage through AI?
03 - Understanding the Workflow

To better understand the problem, I mapped the content discovery journey and spoke with stakeholders responsible for managing campaigns and creative assets. One insight stood out immediately: teams had unintentionally turned people into search engines.

  • Editors became the source of truth for where footage lived, creating bottlenecks that didn't scale
  • Metadata quality varied significantly between projects, making traditional search unreliable
  • Even when footage was located, users had to watch clips individually to judge relevance
04 - Reframing the Opportunity

As I synthesized research findings, I realized the challenge wasn't simply improving search. The real opportunity was reducing the gap between discovery and creation.

Creative teams didn't just want to find footage. They wanted to immediately use it.

This shift in thinking transformed the project from a media management tool into an AI-powered creative workflow.

05 - Introducing HeroFlow

An AI-powered video intelligence platform that helps teams discover, understand, and repurpose content through natural language.

Rather than forcing users to navigate folders and metadata manually, HeroFlow understands footage the same way users describe it.

🔍
Semantic Search

Users search naturally, like "show me happy family moments from our summer campaign", and the platform surfaces relevant clips without manual tags.

📋
HeroFlow

AI-generated insights for every clip, scene description, emotional tone, subjects, and brand relevance, so teams evaluate content instantly.

✂️
Prompt-Based Editing

From a creative brief, users generate a draft edit assembled from relevant footage, shrinking the gap between discovery and execution.

06 - End-to-End User Journey
01
Search

"Find lifestyle footage featuring families outdoors."

02
Discover

Semantic Search surfaces relevant clips across thousands of hours.

03
Understand

HeroFlow surfaces metadata and contextual insights per clip.

04
Create

User selects footage and generates a new edit through a prompt.

05
Ship

A reusable draft asset is ready within minutes, not hours.

07 - Key Design Decisions

Throughout the sprint, one challenge surfaced repeatedly: trust. AI-generated metadata is valuable only if users believe it.

✅
Editable Metadata

Users can correct AI-generated tags, keeping the system accurate over time.

📊
Confidence Indicators

Visibility into how certain the system is about its recommendations.

🔎
Search-First

Advanced functionality stays secondary, keeping the platform approachable.

⚖️
AI + Control

These decisions balanced automation with user control throughout.

08 - Impact

The concept positioned Lemonlight's growing content archive as a strategic asset rather than a storage problem. By combining discovery, contextual understanding, and content creation into a single workflow, HeroFlow demonstrated how AI could dramatically reduce the effort required to reuse existing footage.

Success Metrics

⏱️
6 hrs → 10 min

Target reduction in footage retrieval time per campaign.

📈
DAU / WAU

Daily and weekly active users as primary retention signals.

⭐
NPS Score

Net Promoter Score from agency and brand teams after onboarding.

♻️
Reuse Rate

Increase in repurposed video assets vs. newly produced content.

The concept was ultimately selected as the foundation for future exploration of Lemonlight's AI-powered content ecosystem.

09 - What I Learned
"The most important challenge wasn't building AI features, it was designing trust around them. Successful AI experiences aren't defined by how much they automate, they're defined by how effectively they help people make decisions, stay in control, and move faster toward their goals."

By focusing on workflow integration rather than AI for its own sake, I was able to design a solution that felt both innovative and practical for creative teams.
